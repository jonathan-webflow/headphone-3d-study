01The brief

Take a product apart without a screwdriver.

The goal was a study, not a product page. Build a headphone in 3D accurate enough to take apart, and let the visitor do it by scrolling.

Every layer had to be a real part: glass, bezel, graphic plate, board, driver, battery, cushion. Nothing painted on, nothing faked for the camera.

03Look development

Tuned one render at a time.

Graphite is hard to light: too soft and it reads as grey plastic, too hard and it turns silver. Narrow strip lights draw the edges and leave the faces dark.

01Wireframe & topology

Hard-surface parts are light cages with a Bevel modifier, so edges catch the light without dense geometry. Shells, rims, trays and glass add Weighted Normal to keep their large faces flat. The cushions are plain quad grids that follow the foam.

02Materials & textures

Principled BSDF only, with the same values the site uses in Three.js, so the films and the browser read the same. Relief comes from tiling normal maps on the first UV set; sheen does the work on leather, foam and fabric.
